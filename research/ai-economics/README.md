# AI Economics: Corporate Profitability vs. Societal Costs

Independent white paper · Research initiated by Ivan Popov · November 2026

## Research question

Can AI adoption improve corporate profitability while weakening consumer demand and public finances? This study examines Russia as a case, including the cost of technology ownership and sanctions-related constraints.

## Key findings

Published estimates cited in the paper suggest annual business benefits of €7.54 billion (RUB 680 billion) in 2026, rising to €19.95–28.81 billion (RUB 1.8–2.6 trillion) in one to three years. A 2025 survey reports that 47% of large firms considered workforce reductions when adopting AI; this measures intentions, not actual layoffs.

The following are illustrative annual scenarios, not forecasts (EUR billion, rounded):

| Scenario | Displaced workers | Immediate re-employment | Months without work | Pre-tax profit | Direct fiscal balance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Favourable | 500,000 | 70% | 3 | +23.27 | +5.43 |
| Base | 1,000,000 | 30% | 9 | +12.74 | −0.68 |
| Adverse | 2,000,000 | 10% | 12 | −1.88 | −12.60 |

At base-case profit and unchanged assumptions, the paper estimates a fiscal break-even displacement threshold of approximately 824,000 workers.

The base-case annual TCO is €7.20 billion (RUB 650 billion). EUR equivalents use the Bank of Russia rate cited in the English edition: EUR 1 = RUB 90.2397 on 26 November 2026. Display values are rounded; model calculations remain in RUB. The Russian edition retains RUB figures.

## Methodology

The study combines published estimates and survey data with illustrative total-cost-of-ownership modelling, three scenarios and a break-even sensitivity calculation. Gross benefits less annual TCO yield pre-tax profit. TCO includes hardware depreciation, staffing, licences and cloud, integration, sanctions-related incremental costs, security, energy and training.

Lost gross wages equal displacement × (1 − immediate re-employment rate) × RUB 103,000 × months without work. Net income is assumed to be 87% of gross wages and consumption 80% of net income. Direct fiscal balance equals 25% of positive profit minus 43% of lost gross wages and RUB 100,000 per worker not immediately re-employed.

## Limitations

The paper is not peer-reviewed; primary data and full source methodologies have not been independently verified. There is no established national count of AI-caused layoffs in the study. Scenarios are not predictions or GDP estimates. VAT, severance, new jobs, tax relief, multipliers and taxes paid by AI staff are excluded. Overlapping measures must not be added as GDP damage. If published benefits are already net, TCO must not be deducted again.
